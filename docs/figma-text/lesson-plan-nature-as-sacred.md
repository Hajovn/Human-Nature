## About this lesson

- **about**: `Nature as Sacred explores the idea that nature can be understood in more than one way. It invites children to connect scientific knowledge with cultural meaning and inner experience.`
- **description** (LessonCard line, from the resource-library card): `Nature as Sacred explores the idea that nature can be understood in more than one way.`

### Students learn to (**goals**)

1. `Reflection`
2. `Respect for local Sasak worldviews`
3. `Awareness of one's own thoughts and feelings`

## Lesson context (**context**)

Two paragraphs (Figma separates them with a line-separator character U+2028; use a normal paragraph break):

`This lesson takes place at the beach. If this is not possible, it can also be held in another natural area near the school, such as a grassy field.`

`Children explore the sea through both scientific explanation and cultural storytelling. By observing their surroundings, listening to stories, and reflecting quietly, they learn that nature can be understood in different ways and that learning can happen through presence and experience.`

## The lesson in four steps (**steps**)

1. **title** `Arrival & observation`
   **text** `Children sit in a circle at the beach and share what they see, hear, and notice.`
2. **title** `Introduction`
   **text** `Today's lesson will be a little different. We will learn through two stories about this place. One story comes from science, and one comes from a princess who lived here long ago.`
3. **title** `Reading through two stories`
   **text** `Using illustrated cards, the teacher explains:\nhow tides work (scientific perspective)\nwhat the sea represents in Sasak culture (values and respect)`
   (Lines 2–3 are a list in the design.)
4. **title** `Silent reflection`
   **text** `Children reflect individually in a small diary through drawing or writing, followed by optional sharing.`

## Key message / Teacher tips

The label here is **"Teacher Tips"** (plural) instead of "Teacher Tip"; the site uses one interface label for all lesson plans, so keep "Teacher tip".

- **keyMessage**: `This lesson helps children explore nature with both their mind and their inner awareness. There are no right or wrong answers.`
- **teacherTip**: `Use a calm voice and slow pacing.\nEncourage listening; to the story, to nature, and to each other.\nSharing is always voluntary.\nPresent the story as "a story people tell", not as fact.\nEmphasize that science explains how, while stories help us think about meaning.` (five lines; a list in the design)
